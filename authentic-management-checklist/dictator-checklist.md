/**
 * A checklist for avoiding the "Dictator in the Room" failure mode.
 *
 * Based on the pattern where authority in the room kills productive decision-making:
 * a leader litigates a vetted proposal for an hour, designs a worse solution
 * at the whiteboard, and the team silently complies — then builds the wrong thing.
 */

## How to Not Be "The Dictator in the Room"

**On arriving prepared:**
- [ ] Read the pre-read. If you have questions, ask them before the meeting — not as a stall during it
- [ ] If you walked in skeptical, name that upfront — don't bury it in an hour of questions

**On knowing what the meeting is for:**
- [ ] When a meeting is a decision meeting, make the decision — don't turn it into a discovery session
- [ ] If you want to redesign the solution, call a separate meeting for that; don't hijack the one in progress
- [ ] Recognize the difference between "I have a clarifying question" and "I am not ready to let this move forward"

**On the whiteboard moment:**
- [ ] If you find yourself at the whiteboard designing a new feature in real time — stop. That is not a review, that is a takeover
- [ ] Proposals vetted by the people who will build it carry weight. Your whiteboard sketch does not, yet
- [ ] If the group's energy shifts from engaged to compliant, that is a signal you have stopped leading and started dictating

**On silence in the room:**
- [ ] If no one is pushing back on your idea, that is not agreement — that is people calculating the cost of disagreeing with you
- [ ] Create the conditions where someone can say "I think that's wrong" without it ending badly for them
- [ ] After the meeting, ask someone you trust: "Was that the right call?" and actually want the answer

**The hard one:**
- [ ] Authority gets you compliance. It does not get you the best outcome. The fact that everyone went and built the wrong thing anyway is on you, not them
